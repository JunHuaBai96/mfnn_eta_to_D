                                                                               Viscosity and diffusivity, two important transport coefﬁcients, are systemati-
                                                                               cally investigated from unary melt to binary to multicomponent melts in the
                                                                               present work. By coupling with Kaptay’s viscosity equation of pure liquid
                                                                               metals and effective radii of diffusion species, the Sutherland equation is
                                                                               modiﬁed by taking the size effect into account, and further derived into an
                                                                               Arrhenius formula for the convenient usage. Its reliability for predicting
                                                                               self-diffusivity and impurity diffusivity in unary liquids is then validated by
                                                                               comparing the calculated self-diffusivities and impurity diffusivities in liquid
                                                                               Al- and Fe-based alloys with the experimental and the assessed data. More-
                                                                               over, the Kozlov model was chosen among various viscosity models as the
                                                                               most reliable one to reproduce the experimental viscosities in binary and
                                                                               multicomponent melts. Based on the reliable viscosities calculated from the
                                                                               Kozlov model, the modiﬁed Sutherland equation is utilized to predict the tra-
                                                                               cer diffusivities in binary and multicomponent melts, and validated in Al–Cu,
                                                                               Al–Ni and Al–Ce–Ni melts. Comprehensive comparisons between the
                                                                               calculated results and the literature data indicate that the experimental tracer
                                                                               diffusivities and the theoretical ones can be well reproduced by the present
                                                                               calculations. In addition, the vacancy-wind factor in binary liquid Al–Ni
                                                                               alloys with the increasing temperature is also discussed. What’s more, the
                                                                               calculated inter-diffusivities in liquid Al–Cu, Al–Ni and Al–Ag–Cu alloys are
                                                                               also in excellent agreement with the measured and theoretical data. Compari-
                                                                               sons between the simulated concentration proﬁles and the measured ones in
                                                                               Al–Cu, Al–Ce–Ni and Al–Ag–Cu melts are further used to validate the
                                                                               present calculation method.
                                                                               Keywords: transport properties; diffusion; liquids; Sutherland equation


                                                                   1. Introduction
                                                                   Viscosity and diffusivity in melts, as two important transport coefﬁcients, are key
                                                                   parameters which largely inﬂuence the structure/microstructure evolution during casting
                                                                   and crystal growth [1,2]. Moreover, viscosity and diffusivity are also in close relation-
                                                                   ship with the kinetic fragility and the glass-forming ability of liquids in a wide variety
                                                                   of materials (i.e. metallic alloys, organic and inorganic compounds, colloids) [3].

                                                                   However, reliable experimental information on viscosity and diffusivity of metallic
                                                                   liquid is scarce due to the effects of buoyancy-driven convective ﬂow and chemical
                                                                   reactions between melts and containers [4,5]. The absence of such experimental viscos-
                                                                   ity and diffusivity in metallic liquid leads to the fact that various experimental phenom-
                                                                   ena involving liquid cannot be well theoretically analysed. As a consequence, reliable
                                                                   theoretical data of viscosity and different diffusivities in liquid metals [6–8] are of
                                                                   merit.
                                                                        Numerous theoretical and semi-theoretical studies on viscosity and diffusivity in
                                                                   metallic liquid have been conducted over the past century [8–14]. With the rapid
                                                                   development of computational technology, molecular dynamic (MD) simulations based
                                                                   on the embedded atom method [15–19] and ﬁrst-principles (FP) calculations [20,21]
                                                                   are commonly used to predict the viscosity and diffusivities in liquids. However, even
                                                                   though MD is capable of revealing microscopic mechanism, it still cannot determine
Downloaded by [University of Strathclyde] at 05:28 26 April 2014




                                                                   the accurate absolute values of diffusivities [22]. As for the FP calculations, accurate
                                                                   diffusivities could be obtained but the computation is rather expensive, especially for
                                                                   the calculation of prefactor in the Arrhenius equation. In addition to MD and FP cal-
                                                                   culations, there also exist some semi-theoretical models [11,13] which are less
                                                                   computation-intensive. The empirical parameters of these models are based on the
                                                                   scattered data in the literature [23]. Thus, the diffusivities predicated from those mod-
                                                                   els usually show a large deviation from the recent experimental data [22]. However,
                                                                   the Stokes–Einstein equation [9] is an exceptional one among various semi-theoretical
                                                                   models, and is widely used for viscosity/diffusivity prediction. Strictly speaking, the
                                                                   Stokes–Einstein relation is only valid when the solute atom is larger than the solvent
                                                                   one. When the solute atom is smaller than/equal to the solvent one, the
                                                                   Sutherland–Einstein equation is much more suitable. That is because the size effect is
                                                                   considered in the Sutherland equation [8].
                                                                        Moreover, viscosity corresponds to the mobility in the melts and can be further
                                                                   related to the diffusivity via Einstein’s relation. Once the accurate tracer diffusivity is
                                                                   obtained, the reliable viscosity can be predicted by the Stokes–Einstein or Sutherland–
                                                                   Einstein relation. In reverse, the tracer diffusivity can be also obtained if the viscosity is
                                                                   known.
                                                                        Consequently, in the current work, a systematic study on the viscosity and
                                                                   diffusivity in metallic melts has been performed, aiming at establishing a universal and
                                                                   effective approach to evaluate reliable concentration- and temperature-dependent viscos-
                                                                   ities and diffusivities in liquids from unary to multicomponent systems. Firstly, based
                                                                   on Kaptay’s viscosity equation, a modiﬁed Sutherland equation, in which the size effect
                                                                   is taken into account, has been proposed and derived in an Arrhenius formula for pre-
                                                                   dicting the reliable diffusivity in pure liquids. Then, various models for calculation of
                                                                   viscosities in binary and multicomponent melts are critically reviewed. The most reli-
                                                                   able model for viscosity prediction has been proposed. Tracer diffusivities have been
                                                                   obtained on the basis of the reliable viscosities via the modiﬁed Sutherland equation.
                                                                   After that, the chemical diffusivities in binary and multicomponent melts are further
                                                                   obtained from the calculated tracer diffusivity. Finally, the presently calculated chemical
                                                                   diffusivities are validated by comparing the model-predicted concentration proﬁles in
                                                                   binary and multicomponent liquid diffusion couples with the available experimental
                                                                   ones.

                                                                   4. Summary
                                                                   In the present work, a modiﬁed Sutherland equation was derived by combing it with
                                                                   Kaptay’s viscosity equation of pure liquid metals and temperature-dependent effective
                                                                   diameter to predict both self-diffusivity and impurity diffusivity for metallic liquids. By
                                                                   taking the size effect into account, the modiﬁed Sutherland equation was veriﬁed
                                                                   through comprehensively comparing the calculated impurity diffusivities in different
                                                                   metallic melts with the experimental ones.
                                                                       The viscosities in liquid Al–Cu, Al–Ni and Al–Ag–Cu alloys were calculated by
                                                                   various viscosity models including Kozlov model, Kaptay model, Budai model and
                                                                   Schick model based on the reliable thermodynamic parameters. The comparison
                                                                   between the calculated results from different models and the measured data available in
                                                                   the literature suggested that the Kozlov’s model is the most reliable one. Thus the
                                                                   Sutherland equation in combination with Kozlov model was used to calculate the tracer
                                                                   24                                     W. Chen et al.

                                                                   diffusivities in Al–Cu, Al–Ni and Al–Ce–Ni melts. Comprehensive comparisons
                                                                   between the calculated results and the literature data indicated that the experimental
                                                                   tracer diffusivities and the theoretical ones could be well reproduced by the current
                                                                   results. In addition, the constant M0 in Manning equation for liquid phase was given to
                                                                   be 0.01 for the ﬁrst time according to the previous experimental data. The calculated
                                                                   inter-diffusivities in Al–Cu, Al–Ni and Al–Ag–Cu melts were in excellent agreement
                                                                   with the measured and theoretical data. The presently predicted inter-diffusivities were
                                                                   validated by the excellent agreement between the simulated concentration proﬁles and
                                                                   the measured ones in liquid Al–Cu, Al–Ce–Ni and Al–Ag–Cu diffusion couples.
